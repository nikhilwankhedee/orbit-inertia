================================================================================
1. EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE
================================================================================
  Determine how much of the catastrophic classical DR failure is
  caused by simple gyro scale/bias mismatch versus irreducible
  or context-dependent heading propagation error.

  Three variants tested:
    A0: yaw_rate = gyro_pitch                    (s=1, b=0)
    A1: yaw_rate = s * gyro_pitch                (scale only)
    A2: yaw_rate = s * gyro_pitch + b            (scale + bias)

================================================================================
2. CALIBRATION DATA SELECTION
================================================================================
  Calibration signal: phone gyroscope Pitch (rad/s)
  Reference signal: vehicle CAN yaw rate (deg/sec → rad/s)
  Alignment offset: +1.81 s (applied to reference ONLY,
    for calibration fitting. NOT used during DR propagation.)

  In-sample calibration (diagnostic):
    ALL non-blackout data: 79,796 samples
    NOTE: This has in-sample leakage — same data used for fit and eval.

  Leave-one-window-out calibration (leakage-safe):
    For each blackout window, fit on all non-blackout data
    EXCEPT that window. Mean s=0.1440, mean b=-0.3354 °/s

================================================================================
3. CAUSALITY / LEAKAGE PROTOCOL
================================================================================
  During blackout propagation:
    - NO future information is used.
    - NO vehicle heading/yaw rate is used.
    - NO GPS is used.
    - Only phone IMU + last-known state.

  Calibration parameters (s, b) are:
    - Fixed constants estimated BEFORE the blackout experiment.
    - Do NOT depend on the blackout being evaluated.
    - The leave-one-window-out protocol ensures no blackout window's
      data influences its own calibration parameters.

  The +1.81 s alignment is used ONLY to align the reference yaw rate
  during calibration fitting. It does NOT alter stored timestamps and
  is NOT used during DR propagation.

================================================================================
4. A0/A1/A2 DEFINITIONS
================================================================================
  All variants share identical:
    - Initialization (vehicle reference at blackout start)
    - Velocity model (constant, from vehicle reference)
    - Position propagation (heading + velocity)
    - Blackout selection and evaluation
    - ENU coordinate system

  The ONLY difference is the yaw rate computation:
    A0: yaw_rate = gyro_pitch
    A1: yaw_rate = s * gyro_pitch
    A2: yaw_rate = s * gyro_pitch + b

================================================================================
12. KEY QUESTIONS
================================================================================

  QUESTION 1: Is the current classical DR failure substantially
  explained by gyro scale/bias mismatch?

  ANSWER: NO — calibration reduces 120s final error by only -16.0%.
  The failure is NOT primarily explained by scale/bias mismatch.

  QUESTION 2: After calibration, does heading still become unusable
  during long blackouts?

  ANSWER: YES — heading MAE at 120s is 71.2° after calibration.
  Heading propagation remains catastrophic even with scale/bias correction.

  QUESTION 3: Does substantial position drift remain after calibration?

  ANSWER: YES — 120s final error is 986.1 m after calibration.
  Substantial position drift persists.

  QUESTION 4: What is the strongest evidence that a learned residual
  correction is worth testing?

  ANSWER: The remaining drift after calibration (986.1 m at 120s)
  demonstrates that scale/bias correction alone is insufficient.
  Context-dependent, nonlinear heading error correction is warranted.
  A learned residual model can target the irreducible heading error
  that linear calibration cannot address.

  Calibration ablation confirms that scale/bias correction alone
  does not solve the navigation problem.
